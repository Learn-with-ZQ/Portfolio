Stage 1 of 6 · Greige fabric

Stage 1 of 6

Greige fabric

Greige is cloth in its loom or knitting-machine state: unbleached, unfinished, still carrying loose surface fibres and sizing. Nothing has been done to it yet, which is why it is where an integrated mill starts.

Comes in as
Raw cloth, off-white to oatmeal, with a slightly hairy surface.
Goes on to
Preparation, beginning with singeing.

Stage 2 of 6

Singeing

The fabric runs quickly past a flame or heated surface that burns off the protruding fibres without scorching the cloth. A clean surface takes dye more evenly and pills less later on.

Changes
Surface fuzz removed; the weave reads clearly.
Why it matters
Even shade and a smoother hand in the finished piece.

Stage 3 of 6

Mercerizing

Cotton is treated with caustic soda while held under tension. The fibres swell and round out, so the cloth gains lustre and strength and absorbs dye more readily.

Changes
A visible sheen; better dye uptake.
Why it matters
Deeper colour from the same dye, and a more durable fabric.

Stage 4 of 6

Dyeing

Prepared fabric is dyed to the shade the order calls for, then washed and dried. From here on, the cloth carries its colour.

Changes
Greige becomes coloured cloth.
Goes on to
The cutting table.

Singeing, mercerizing and dyeing are also listed as services for commercial dye houses, so fabric preparation can be enquired about on its own.

Stage 5 of 6

Cutting & stitching

Fabric is laid, cut to pattern and sewn: the cut-make-trim work. On record, the products leaving this stage include garments, hats, cushions, sports socks and knitted socks.

Changes
Cloth becomes finished pieces.
You supply
Your tech pack, sizes and trims list.

Packed for export
